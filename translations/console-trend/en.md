In 2025, it turns out, the 3 leading AI companies released console tools for programmers: Anthropic's Claude Code in February, OpenAI's Codex CLI in April, Google's Gemini CLI in June.

In the '70s and '80s the command line was a breakthrough (for specialists), and back then there was no choice: out of sheer necessity and curiosity about the new technology, users eagerly typed in commands copied from magazines. But in the '90s, when most people started getting into computers, the console became torture for them — not an interface but a dull foreign-language course with a dreary backdrop: new words, grammar, rules and exceptions, all of it as text on a monochrome screen. Few were willing to learn it well enough to work efficiently and never trade it for anything afterwards — same as with touch typing.

Knowing this, clever marketers slipped us the GUI — it's a sign language! A way to talk to the computer the way a tourist talks to a waiter abroad, jabbing a finger at the pictures on the menu. Even the jabbing skill was introduced to us gently: Microsoft thoughtfully put Solitaire into Windows to get us used to dragging with the mouse, and Minesweeper to master both the left and the right mouse buttons.

I do touch-type, and yet I don't like the console. A prejudice instilled in my formative years holds firm to this day.

Meanwhile, to spare us humans the effort, the computer spent all this time trying to learn our language: tooltips, T9, Siri, the paperclip in Office — they tried so hard, and everyone hated them.

And suddenly the machine began to understand normal speech, and with it the console came back. After 30 years we finally have an interface that's no worse than vim! The waiter simply learned our language — and the pictures on the menu were no longer needed.

Isn't that why Markdown is flourishing today, as buttons lose ground? In the 2000s the standard was Word and no other text editor, thanks to its unbeatable argument: spell check. Then spell check showed up in "any input field", and you could format text anywhere (sometimes even without buttons). It's as if those awful '80s were back, when my mom typed non-printing characters straight into the text in some DOS editor to print a formatted page — only now it somehow isn't that scary!? Don't be fooled: the only people it doesn't scare are specialists and those who have familiar little buttons next to "any input field".

I guess I'm just in a bubble: I'm surrounded by people who can hook an agent up (with the help of an agent) to the services they need and build a "smart single window" for accessing everything, and they like the console. So the console is back in fashion for specialists, along with the good old Unix philosophy: small tools, pipes, sequences and a common text interface? (Just like in the '80s?) Wait a minute — for them it never went away!

As for ordinary users and me, we'll have little windows and little buttons, little crutches and handrails!

Are you already on your way to your own "smart single window" with agents at work?
