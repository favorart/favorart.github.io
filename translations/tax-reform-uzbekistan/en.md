The new tax office doesn't expect you to do things right; everything is set up so that you simply can't get it wrong.

The days when a sole proprietor had to keep their own independent books and spend effort proving their honesty to the state are fading away along with the scarcity of data. Through online services and payments, electronic receipts and cash registers, centralized data processing and statistical predictive models, the tax office sees transactions in real time and already knows more about you than you do. In this new environment, a personal accountant for a small business (one without complex internal financial dependencies) looks like nothing more than an unnecessary administrative burden. A company is opened electronically, and everything is heading toward a point where running a business won't require dealing with government agencies in person.

Is Uzbekistan really going further than everyone else with its tax reform? What's the idea behind it? All sole proprietors are moving to a single turnover tax, and payments are recorded via smart QR codes, even without physical cash registers. On the one hand, the turnover tax rate was cut from 4% to 1%, and the threshold for mandatory manual reporting was raised to ~$82,000 [1]. On the other hand, all preferential programs were scrapped, which increased the burden on the smallest and most vulnerable businesses (the former self-employed), who used to pay a token $50 a year in social tax. As I see it, the goal is to bring cash payments out of the shadows and tighten tax discipline. To make the system work, fintech platforms (for example, Paynet Xolis), along with banks, are becoming tax agents and building tools into their apps for receiving and sending payments via QR codes. The scheme is simple: at payment, the amount is split automatically — 99% goes to the seller, and the 1% tax goes straight to the state budget.

At its core is Continuous Transaction Controls, a concept well known around the world that lets the state be present at every cashless transaction (crypto excluded) [2]. My hypothesis that a Chinese system had been adopted off the shelf turned out to be wrong. That said, Uzbekistan's State Tax Committee did officially discuss introducing analogues of the Golden Tax system (developed by Huawei and CEIEC). In China, Golden Tax Phase IV is still just real-time monitoring of invoices ("fapiao"), bank data and transactions. So automatic tax deduction turns out to be Uzbekistan's own innovation.

This system drops the hypocritical masks, since even before you couldn't get away without paying a known amount of tax. Now tax evasion turns from a crime into a technically impossible bug.

The cherry on top is the government's promise to freeze any other changes to tax policy until 2030, so as not to push people into the gray zone. We'll see whether they manage to resist the temptation and not raise taxes for another 10 long years!

[1] - This isn't the highest ceiling: in Georgia, for example, with a similar 1% rate, the threshold for switching to full bookkeeping is much higher — around $180,000.

[2] - That said, the EU is already introducing DAC8, which obliges crypto exchanges to report residents' transactions, making these transactions transparent to tax authorities.
