\#How did you know the world had changed and LLMs had come into your life?

A few years ago I got the chance to try my hand at teaching.

My first teaching experience was magical:

The university sent me on a business trip, and I flew out full of anticipation. I had spent months preparing, building a near-perfect syllabus for my course and thinking through every topic. Naturally, I didn't finish in time. In the evenings I wrote up the remaining topics, prepared and rehearsed, and the next morning I met the students face to face and did my best to present the material and get them hooked. It was the final year of their master's, and my goal was simple and honest: help the students build a decent pet project so they wouldn't sink on their first plunge into the cold waters of the job market. I could feel genuine interest. I came home recharged with fresh energy from the students.

Then, for some reason, the university ran out of money for business trips. The next course had to be taught online, and it barely resembled teaching anymore. I still gave lectures, ran seminars and tried to give it everything, like the last time, but the feedback made it plain: the students weren't taking the subject seriously and, worse, had no desire to. The pay for an online course turned out to be noticeably lower. And in the end the final assessment boiled down to tests.

Anyway, back to the topic at hand!

In the year BEFORE the all-knowing agents arrived, I could feel the students' strain in their answers to written questions and tests: some were trying to figure things out, some were gaming the system, some were trying to team up and find the right solution, some gave up.

In the year AFTER the all-knowing agents arrived, the answers became wordy and shallow, the strain disappeared, and nobody even tried to read the question themselves.

I pretended to teach; the students pretended to learn.

\#Moral

Why does a student have neither the time nor the desire to learn?

Why does a professor have neither the time nor the desire to teach?

The meaning has gone missing from many kinds of work, so how do we find it again?

P.S.: Just out of curiosity, I fed this text to an LLM and asked it to write the moral. It pretty much nailed it.
